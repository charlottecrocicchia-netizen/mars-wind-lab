# Where the evidence stands

**Synthesis · 28 September 2026 · after six declared tests, an independent audit and five follow-up calculations.** This page states what the project has established, what it has constrained, what it has found untestable with the public data at hand, and which measurements would settle the remaining questions. Numbers are taken from the saved outputs cited on each linked page; nothing here is a new calculation.

## The question

The southern highlands carry strong crustal magnetic anomalies; the northern lowlands carry weak ones. Two families of explanation compete: the ancient dynamo produced a stronger field over the south, or the same field was recorded and preserved differently on each side. The project set out to find calculations on public data that could favour or exclude parts of either family.

## Established

- **The magnetic contrast is robust.** The southern mean field at 150 km is 3.8 times the northern mean, and between 3.1 and 4.5 for every tested definition of the two sides. ([First diagnostics](FIRST_RESULTS.md))
- **The crustal thickness contrast is robust in sign but not in size.** Our own gravity inversion, validated against the published archive to 4.3 km RMS, gives a south-minus-north contrast of 24 km at equal density and 8 km with the newest density estimates; the declared scenarios span 7 to 41 km. Every origin model calibrated to a fixed contrast inherits this uncertainty. ([Test 6](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md))
- **Slowly cooled crust under a frequently reversing dynamo keeps a few percent of its record.** In the accepted conductive histories, crust at 30 km takes about 770 Myr to cool through the magnetite blocking band; random reversals every 0.67 Myr leave a median 2% (95th percentile 6%). ([Test 4](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md))
- **The orbital field model cannot be extrapolated to a point on the ground.** At the two checked sites it is five times too high (Zhurong) and six times too low (InSight). Every crater-scale magnetic claim from orbit, including this project's early regional pilot, inherits that limit. ([Test 5](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md))
- **A single rapidly cooled body escapes cancellation; a stack built over many chrons does not.** Bodies up to about 3 km thick cool through the magnetite band within one chron of 0.67 Myr; a hundred thin bodies emplaced under such a dynamo retain about a quarter of their summed magnetization when built over 10 Myr and about an eighth over 100 Myr. ([Rapidly cooled bodies](RAPID_BODIES.md))

## Constrained, not decided

- **A deep southern archive inherited from before 4.1 Ga is difficult in conductive histories.** At 30 km the magnetite Curie point was last crossed at a median 4.09 Ga (39% of histories before 4.1 Ga, 59% before 3.7 Ga); pyrrhotite below 25 km never cooled in time. The shallow crust is unconstrained by this model. ([Test 2](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md))
- **The magnetization needed is several amperes per metre over 20 km in a declared cylinder geometry**, tens of times more if acquired during slow cooling under reversals. A low southern density does not exclude this: with efficient single-domain magnetite, 4 A/m needs 0.05 to 0.1% magnetite; even the cancellation-corrected 208 A/m needs 2.5 to 5%. Density is not a discriminant. ([Test 3](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md), [Density and remanence](DENSITY_REMANENCE.md))
- **Surface age carries out-of-region information about the field, but so does the hemisphere.** Age alone does not remove the hemispheric difference; at equal Noachian epoch the north stays two to four times weaker except in a small, geographically concentrated sample. ([Test 1](DISCRIMINATING_TESTS.md), [Boundary transects](BOUNDARY_WALK.md))

## Not testable with the data at hand

- **Whether the boundary is a magnetic discontinuity at equal surface age.** Matched Arabia Terra controls and boundary transects both fail their declared support checks: at 2° there is almost no Noachian terrain on both sides of the boundary at the same place. ([Arabia preflight](ARABIA_PREFLIGHT.md), [Boundary transects](BOUNDARY_WALK.md))
- **Whether northern source depths follow surface age.** The rank correlation between equivalent source depth and Noachian fraction is not stable across the declared sensitivities in either hemisphere; the direction in the north, if anything, is opposite to the resurfacing prediction, on ten windows. ([Depth and age](DEPTH_AGE.md))
- **The carrier mineral and cooling rate of any actual southern source.** Every constraint above is conditional on declared carriers, blocking bands and thermal histories.

## What this leaves

The recording-and-preservation explanation survives as one part of the story, and it now has a specific shape: if the early dynamo reversed as often as the basin record suggests, the strong southern sources were built quickly, within single chrons, or during intervals of stable polarity, or they carry far more magnetization than a coherent source needs. A genuine difference between the hemispheres at equal surface age has not been removed by any calculation here. Neither family is excluded.

## The measurements that would settle it

1. **A magnetometer on the ground on Noachian terrain north of the boundary.** Old northern rock as magnetized as old southern rock would decide the age-versus-hemisphere question that the orbital map cannot.
2. **A seismic constraint on crustal density in the south.** It would fix the thickness contrast between 7 and 41 km and remove the largest uncertainty in every heat-budget and origin model.
3. **A reversal chronology of the Martian dynamo between 4.5 and 3.7 Ga**, from oriented, dated samples. The coherence calculations turn any reversal rate into a required emplacement time for the southern sources.
4. **Oriented samples from a strong southern anomaly**, for the carrier mineral, grain state and cooling rate.
