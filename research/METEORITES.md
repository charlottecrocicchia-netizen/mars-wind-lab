# Meteorite magnetism and the dichotomy

The useful question is **what field was recorded, when, by which mineral, and where on Mars?** A bulk magnetization value without those qualifications cannot discriminate a northern impact from southern crustal growth.

## Four kinds of information that must remain separate

| Quantity | What it tells us | What it does not establish alone |
|---|---|---|
| Magnetic mineralogy, susceptibility, hysteresis | Carrier identity, concentration and capacity to retain magnetization | Ancient field strength |
| Natural remanent magnetization, after demagnetization | Surviving components and their relative directions | Martian origin or acquisition age of every component |
| Laboratory paleointensity | An estimate conditional on a suitable recording mechanism and calibration | Whether the field came from a global dynamo or nearby magnetized crust |
| Geological provenance and chronology | Candidate source, crystallization and subsequent events | Absolute geographic orientation, or equivalence between formation and remanence ages |

Carrier differences across meteorite families are documented by [Rochette et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1945-5100.2005.tb00961.x). Geological and sampling context is reviewed by [Udry et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006523). This project's proposed extraction table below turns these distinctions into explicit data fields.

## Priority sample families

| Sample / family | Source result | Connection to dichotomy | Main restriction |
|---|---|---|---|
| ALH 84001 | Ancient paleointensity and mineral-scale evidence interpreted as polarity reversals | Early field behaviour and preservation through impacts | Uncertain geographic source, acquisition/impact chronology and overprints |
| MIL 03346, a nakhlite | Laboratory tests support an approximately 5 μT field record | Demonstrates later recording of a weak field | A local crustal field can explain it without a late global dynamo |
| Tissint, a shergottite fall | Weak post-shock remanence interpreted as a local Martian field | Links shock, cooling and preserved crustal fields | Crystallization, shock and recording ages are different questions |
| NWA 7034 and paired regolith breccias | Abundant magnetic oxides; studied stones remagnetized by hand magnets | Candidate carrier-rich crustal analogue and ancient lithological record | Natural remanence of contaminated stones cannot supply an ancient Martian paleointensity |
| Other shergottites / nakhlites / chassignites | Petrology, carriers, isotopic reservoirs and thermal histories | Independent constraints on crust–mantle differentiation | No inference of pristine paleomagnetism without sample-specific tests |
| Perseverance contextualized samples | Potential magnetic carriers identified remotely | Future laboratory tests could combine orientation, stratigraphy and mineralogy | Candidate laboratory science, not a magnetic measurement on Earth already obtained |

Sources: ALH [Weiss et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1029/2008GL035585), [Steele et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ade9071); MIL [2021 study](https://doi.org/10.1029/2021JE006856); Tissint [Gattacceca et al., 2013](https://doi.org/10.1111/maps.12172); NWA [Gattacceca et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014GL060464), [Vervelidou et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007464); Perseverance [Mansbach et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JE008505).

The hand-magnet result concerns nine measured paired stones. It is not a license to assume that every future NWA-family specimen has the same contamination history, nor to discard useful chemistry and mineralogy. A new candidate needs an independently documented chain of custody and preservation tests. Removing an outer layer is not a generic guarantee that the interior is pristine. [Vervelidou et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007464).

## An explicit record for every sample

The proposed unit of analysis is a **specimen/component**, nested within a pairing group and a possible launch event. Store:

- Official name and specimen identifier; pairing and launch-group identifiers; repository and primary DOI.
- Lithology and magnetic carriers, grain sizes, coercivity/unblocking spectra and measurement orientation.
- Collection history: witnessed fall or find, weathering, handling, documented exposure to magnets, and laboratory field history.
- Crystallization age, alteration age, shock/resetting age, remanence age, ejection age and terrestrial residence, each with method and uncertainty. Leave unknown values unknown.
- Proposed recording process: thermal, chemical, depositional, viscous, shock-related or unresolved; mixed components remain separate.
- Paleointensity method, alteration checks, anisotropy/cooling-rate corrections where applicable, uncertainty and failed as well as successful experiments.
- Tests of antiquity and stability, such as baked-contact or conglomerate constraints when the material permits them.
- Candidate Martian source region and its evidence, confidence and alternative source assignments. Do not convert a crater hypothesis into an exact measured coordinate.

This is a data schema for the next laboratory-literature extraction, **not a filled database of all specimens**. The present catalog indexes papers. The [Meteoritical Bulletin](https://www.lpi.usra.edu/meteor/metbull.php?sea=Martian) supplies authoritative nomenclature and classifications; its entries are not all independent falls or pristine magnetic recorders.

## Why returned samples matter

Meteorites usually lack original orientation and stratigraphic context, and their ages and lithologies are unevenly sampled. A contextualized returned suite could support tests of remanence stability, magnetic mineral formation and field evolution unavailable from an orbital map. It would still sample a limited region. The preservation-test diagrams and sample limitations in [Weiss et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404259121) are a useful experimental guide, not a claim that sample return has already delivered those results.

## Connecting samples to a global model

Treat the connection as a chain: **crust production → mineral formation → field acquisition → alteration/impact → ejection/collection → measurement**. Each step can erase or transform information. Source-crater work can help connect the chain to a province, but it is a model with uncertainty. [Lagain et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-022-31444-8); [Herd et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adn2378).

A first useful analysis would compare permitted magnetization ranges for the observed carrier assemblages with the magnetization required by orbital forward models. Re-run it under alternative source depths, recording fields and preservation histories. If a model needs a large volume of carrier-rich material unsupported by petrology, that is a specific tension worth investigating. If several combinations fit, report the degeneracy rather than selecting a unique dynamo history.

No new paleointensity, provenance assignment or magnetization inversion is computed in this release. Its contribution is to make the valid comparisons and missing measurements explicit.
