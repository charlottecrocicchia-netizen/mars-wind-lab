# From the September review to testable questions

**Targeted review · 27 September 2026.** This note assesses selected claims from the research overview supplied by Charlotte. It extends the [scientific synthesis](SYNTHESIS.md); it is not a verification of every reference in that overview, a systematic review, or a new numerical result.

## What the overview adds

The dichotomy is a useful organizing question for this project. Its origin and its influence on where we can observe ancient rocks are distinct problems. Connecting them is productive only when each link keeps its spatial scale, epoch and assumptions.

The active reading route connects crustal structure, seismic and tidal constraints, magnetic memory and meteorite provenance. The Udry et al. (2025) review adds useful qualifications on meteorite sampling. Records retain their reading-depth labels; adding a citation does not certify its methods or data.

## Claims to retain, qualify or correct

| Topic | Assessment and wording to use | Primary source checked |
|---|---|---|
| A warmer southern mantle settles the origin debate | **Qualify.** The inferred 200–400 °C contrast concerns the present mantle and depends on rheology and composition. Berne et al. discuss convection, crustal insulation and a hybrid involving a northern impact. This does not establish that Borealis has ceased to be viable or rank all formation models. | [Berne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x), selected interpretation passages |
| Crustal density is identical north and south | **Correct.** Kim et al. exclude most tested crustal models with contrasts above 200 kg/m³ and obtain a mean thickness of 42–56 km. Similar density is not zero contrast or proof of identical composition. Keep the tested model family with the numbers. | [Kim et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL103482), abstract and crustal-model comparison |
| Southern attenuation supports an internal origin | **Retain with attribution.** Sun and Tkalčić report Q = 481–543 for Terra Cimmeria versus 800–2,000 for Cerberus Fossae and argue for an endogenic interpretation. These are regional path estimates, not direct temperatures or complete hemispheric coverage. Cite the 2025 volume; online publication was 27 December 2024. | [Sun and Tkalčić](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL110921), abstract and publication history |
| The smaller core in the 2023 layered model has a diameter near 3,600 km | **Correct the model attribution.** Khan et al. report a metallic-core radius of 1,675 ± 30 km and a silicate layer 150 ± 15 km thick. Doubling that radius gives a diameter of 3,350 ± 60 km. Do not combine radii from different inversion families into one interior model. | [Khan et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06586-4), author-hosted abstract |
| The 2025 inner-core correction changes the detected radius | **Clarify.** The correction replaces a reference in the Methods discussion of core composition. It does not revise the radius. The physical interpretation can still be assessed critically, but the existence of this correction is not evidence against it. | [Bi et al., correction](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09981-1), correction notice |
| Ancient zircons directly date the dichotomy | **Qualify.** Bouvier et al. distinguish zircon crystallization from the earlier extraction of their source reservoir. Connecting either age to a global hemispheric boundary requires an additional geological argument. Build a chronology with separate event types. | [Bouvier et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0222-z), abstract |
| A meteorite count measures independent spatial coverage | **Qualify.** Udry et al. emphasize missing outcrop context and age/compositional selection. The supplied PNAS DOI is valid and belongs to the 2025 volume, not a distinct 2024 review. Do not merge counts using different pairing definitions. | [Udry et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404254121), inventory-limitations section and metadata |

The overview's strongest causal chains should therefore become **hypotheses with alternative branches**, not a single established history. Our project inference is that common tests are more useful than counting papers in favour of a mechanism. Several publications may share observations or model assumptions.
