# A working hypothesis for the Martian dichotomy

**Charlotte Crocicchia's Mars research project · Version 1 · 24 September 2026**

**Research question: can one history of crustal growth, cooling and magnetic recording explain the structural and magnetic dichotomies while remaining compatible with the present interior?**

This is a proposed research argument, not a fitted model or a discovery. It selects a tractable question from the [evidence synthesis](SYNTHESIS.md). The website is an interface to this argument; the observations and explicit assumptions must determine the outcome.

## The idea to pursue

**Working hypothesis: an early contrast in crustal structure helped create two different thermal and magnetic archives. Part of the magnetic dichotomy could therefore arise from when, where and how rocks recorded a shared planetary dynamo, without requiring a hemispherically stronger ancient field.**

“Shared” means a common dynamo history with an explicit field geometry; even a dipole does not produce the same field strength at every location. This baseline does not assume that all southern rocks preserved their first magnetization. Both inheritance and later acquisition must be tested. It is our first model to challenge because it introduces fewer unconstrained field asymmetries, not because the evidence has already selected it.

The initial structural contrast could have been seeded by an impact, internal dynamics or a combination. Its subsequent evolution can partly obscure the trigger. We should first determine which recording histories are physically possible, then ask which origin models can produce them.

## The connections that make this useful

| Evidence and its status | Connection to the working hypothesis | What it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Global gravity/topography interpreted with InSight crustal constraints: a structural contrast, conditional on density and interior models ([Wieczorek et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007298)) | Different crustal columns imply different possible heat budgets and source volumes | A unique impact or convection origin |
| Published forward models link crustal thickness, heat-producing elements, insulation and melt extraction ([Thiriet et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1002/2017JE005431); [Bonnet Gibet et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007472)) | An initial contrast can influence subsequent thermal evolution and crust production | Proof that this feedback dominated Mars, or identification of the original seed |
| Time-variable gravity interpreted as a present mantle asymmetry ([Berne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x)) | Provides an endpoint against which thermal histories can be checked | An ancient crustal temperature measurement or a unique cause |
| Magnetic source depths inferred from localized spectra ([Gong & Wieczorek, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006690)) | Makes source depth, rather than surface field strength alone, central to the thermal test | A directly measured magnetic layer boundary; the inferred depths depend on source geometry |
| Meteorite mineralogy and paleomagnetic preservation tests ([Rochette et al., 2005](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1945-5100.2005.tb00961.x); [Weiss et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404259121)) | Constrains candidate recording materials and which laboratory records can be trusted | A geographically representative sample of the deep magnetic crust |
| Ancient material and later thermal events in NWA 7034; separate remanence constraints from ALH 84001 ([Cassata et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aap8306); [Steele et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ade9071)) | Limits event ordering and provides local tests of recording windows | One globally applicable dichotomy date, or known source coordinates for every specimen |

The causal proposal is **early structural contrast → different thermal/compositional evolution → different acquisition and survival of magnetization → today's orbital signal**. Dynamo history, source geometry and observational resolution enter independently. Later impacts, alteration, burial and erosion can modify several links.

A thick crust can contain more magnetic material, but it can also retain more heat. More crust therefore does not automatically mean more preserved magnetization. Heating can erase a record; cooling while a field is active can create a new one. The discriminant is the **sequence of events at the source depth**, not whether the south is simply “hotter.”

## Two independent choices, four models to compare

These are endmembers for specified regions and source volumes. A heterogeneous planet can contain several histories; allowing an arbitrary mixture in every pixel would make the proposal untestable.

| Model | Dominant recording history | Ancient field | Distinguishing requirement |
|---|---|---|---|
| A — Inherited archive | Early crust records a field, then retains much of that remanence | Shared dynamo history | Deep sources survive subsequent thermal and impact histories |
| B — Rebuilt archive | Much of the original record is reset or new material is emplaced; a later record dominates | Shared dynamo history | Cooling or mineral formation overlaps a field-active interval and leaves sufficient coherent magnetization |
| C — Inheritance plus field asymmetry | As in A | Persistently stronger field in the south during relevant acquisition intervals | A field contrast still improves independent predictions after recording and preservation are constrained |
| D — Rebuilding plus field asymmetry | As in B | Hemispheric field contrast during reacquisition | Both a viable later recording window and the additional field geometry are required |

A and B are our initial tests. C and D are explicit alternatives, not explanations invoked automatically whenever the simpler model misses a point. A hemispheric dynamo must also produce sufficient time-integrated coherence: some published simulations reverse too quickly for strong bulk remanence ([Dietrich & Wicht, 2013](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pepi.2013.01.001)). No probabilities or ranking of these four models have been calculated.

## How this bears on origin scenarios

**Northern excavation:** inherited deep southern sources and loss of northern sources are compatible with this family. But the heating and burial of southern material by ejecta must be modeled too. Magnetic survival constraints have already been applied to this problem ([Citron & Zhong, 2012](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pepi.2012.09.008)). Their assumptions about ejecta and thermal evolution must be revisited before treating their result as an exclusion of every northern impact. A field that persists after cooling permits reacquisition.

**Southern impact and crust production:** a melt-producing event offers a route to thick crust, but a magnetic explanation needs cooling and carrier formation during an available field interval. The impact itself is not an explanation for preserved remanence ([Ballantyne et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115395); [Cheng et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2024.116137)).

**Internal growth and amplification:** crustal growth and thermal feedback can link thickness and later thermal structure. These histories must also preserve old materials or explain their survival as local exceptions; deep intrusions and crustal differentiation can create or reset magnetic carriers. A modern hot mantle is not proof that a plume initiated the contrast ([Bonnet Gibet et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JE008486)).

Our useful constraint would be a set of permitted **growth–heating–cooling–recording sequences**. Each origin model must fit that same set, rather than fitting a different subset of observations.

## The first study: when could the deep southern crust record a field?

Start with Terra Cimmeria and Terra Sirenum, where strong orbital anomalies motivate a source-depth test. Include northern lowland control regions with usable signal; do not interpret a failed depth inversion in a weak-field region as a shallow source.

1. Reproduce regional magnetic spectra at a common altitude and bandwidth. Carry source-depth uncertainty and alternative source geometries; compare more than one field model where feasible.
2. Use an ensemble of crustal thickness/density models and published thermal histories, with explicit carrier hypotheses. Require the same thermal parameters to explain crustal structure and magnetic survival/acquisition. Present mantle constraints are endpoint checks, not imposed ancient geotherms.
3. Determine whether each source-volume history supports early inheritance, later recording, both, or neither. Propagate uncertainty in field-active intervals; do not fix a universally accepted dynamo shutdown date.
4. Forward-predict magnetic observables with cooling-time and polarity cancellation, and test withheld regions. A thermally possible source is not automatically strong enough to explain the observed field.

**First deliverable:** a depth–time compatibility diagram, with an accompanying table of accepted and rejected histories and the observation responsible. No fitted diagram exists yet. The [test protocol](TEST_PROTOCOL.md) defines the inputs, decision rules and failure modes before analysis.

If inheritance fails but a later recording window works, we gain a conditional constraint on remagnetization. If both fail for reasonable carriers and thermal histories, that parameterized crustal history is inadequate. A stronger dynamo cannot rescue a volume that never records or retains remanence. If several histories remain viable, the honest result is a quantified degeneracy and a specification of the additional measurement needed.

## Where meteorites, water and the atmosphere enter

Meteorites constrain mineral carriers, local event sequences and recording processes. NWA 7034 chemistry can remain useful even where hand magnets compromised its natural remanence; the nine tested paired stones are not nine independent Martian regions ([Gattacceca et al., 2014](https://doi.org/10.1002/2014GL060464); [Vervelidou et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007464)). ALH 84001 helps constrain possible field-active intervals, with its own age and recording interpretation. Neither meteorite family supplies a known global field geometry.

Alteration and water can change magnetic carriers; erosion, burial and volcanism can separate the visible boundary from its initial position. These are explicit recording/modification branches, not direct evidence for an ocean-caused crustal dichotomy. Surface mineral abundance is not assumed to represent deep sources ([AlHantoobi et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020GL090379); [McNeil et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-024-01634-8)).

The wind laboratory has a narrower supporting role: surface-pressure harmonics can assess atmospheric loading corrections in time-variable gravity. Present winds do not explain the origin of an ancient crustal contrast. That calculation is a separate sensitivity test and has not yet been implemented; the [research programme](RESEARCH_PLAN.md) specifies the required time and surface conventions.

## What is new to this project, and what is already published?

Thermal control of magnetization, impact survival tests, hemispheric dynamos and crustal feedback are established research directions. [Ruiz, 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2009.05.021) already relates early geotherms to candidate magnetic carriers. The 2026 tidal study itself discusses both thermal recording and dynamo asymmetry. We must not present their synthesis as our discovery.

Our proposed contribution is a **reproducible consistency test using a common set of histories**, explicitly joining source depth, carrier uncertainty, recording chronology and interior constraints. Its scientific novelty remains to be assessed against full texts and existing joint models. The present release ingests geological and magnetic datasets, laboratory archives and orbital mineral maps, and runs descriptive diagnostics. It has not fitted full recording histories or selected an origin mechanism.

## Extension: give mineral formation its own history

The thermal baseline is now complemented by an explicit **fluid–mineral–remanence branch**. Water can create, transform or remove carriers; a later Martian component is not equivalent to terrestrial contamination. The [recording guide](RECORDING.md) distinguishes the relevant processes, and the [water study](WATER.md) defines competing histories and their failure criteria.

The discriminating question becomes: **does the same dated thermal and chemical history explain crustal structure, mineral generations and the surviving magnetic components?** Keep acquisition, alteration, impact, ejection and terrestrial overprint ages separate. In particular, Tissint's secondary record must not be assigned automatically to crystallization, and Lafayette's aqueous alteration date must not be assigned automatically to magnetization.

Our A–D comparison now permits separately constrained thermal and chemical rebuilding within B and D; it does not introduce an unconstrained correction wherever a model fails. Local crustal fields must be considered when younger meteorites record nonzero fields. A global dynamo is only one source-field alternative.

The mineral–magnetism overlay is available, but a valid wet-versus-dry test requires a survey/exposure mask and matched regional controls. An empty spectral cell is not a dry control. This limitation is explicit in the interface and prevents the preliminary overlay from being promoted into an origin claim.
