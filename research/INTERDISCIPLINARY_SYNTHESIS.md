# Mars as one evolving physical system

**Interdisciplinary research synthesis · 28 September 2026 · Charlotte Crocicchia's Mars project**

[Chronology](INTERDISCIPLINARY_CHRONOLOGY.md) · [Magnetic evidence](INTERDISCIPLINARY_MAGNETISM.md) · [Scenarios and tests](INTERDISCIPLINARY_TESTS.md) · [Annotated sources and search coverage](INTERDISCIPLINARY_SOURCES.md)

## The research question

**Which histories of crust formation, differentiation, heat transfer, water-rock reaction and magnetic recording can jointly explain Mars's structural and magnetic dichotomies while satisfying its dated rock record and present interior?**

This is a critical, multidisciplinary literature synthesis and a specification for future tests. It adds source assessments and connections between disciplines; it does not report a new planetary inversion, an inferred complete history or a discovery of the dichotomy's cause. The search is broad and targeted, with its exact coverage and reading depth recorded. It cannot certify that every relevant article, book or language has been covered.

The most useful unit of reasoning is an **event in a place and material**: which process occurred, under what conditions, when, what it changed, and what could survive it. A regional alteration event, a mantle reservoir and a present magnetic anomaly must be connected by an explicit physical and chronological argument.

## What the combined evidence supports

1. **The present contrast has several expressions.** Relief, crustal thickness, density, composition, surface geology and magnetic field do not define interchangeable boundaries. Gravity-derived thickness depends on density and seismic assumptions; an apparently good fit to topography used in that inversion is not independent validation. [Wieczorek et al., 2022](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007298)
2. **A strong anomaly is a record filtered by rock properties and observation geometry.** Its amplitude cannot by itself determine the ancient dynamo strength, the age of acquisition or a unique formation mechanism. Source depths are inferred with model assumptions, and different magnetic products often reuse the same spacecraft measurements. [Langlais et al., 2019](https://doi.org/10.1029/2018JE005854); [Gong and Wieczorek, 2021](https://doi.org/10.1029/2020JE006690)
3. **An early crust and subsequent processing both matter.** Ancient zircon ages and isotope reservoirs constrain material histories, while different meteorite groups can imply different evolutionary paths. A planet-wide scenario must explain survival as well as production and reworking. [Bouvier et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-018-0222-z); [Borg and Kruijer, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404257121)
4. **Water belongs inside the rock and thermal history.** Fluid circulation changes heat transport and mineral assemblages; altered crust can also store water. Those processes need not occur together everywhere or have a single magnetic outcome. [Mittelholz et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JE008832); [Scheller et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abc7717)
5. **Present interior observations are endpoints.** They constrain which histories are possible but do not directly measure the ancient mantle, dynamo or crustal recording temperature. [Berne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x)

## The connections to investigate

| Physical link | What must be carried between disciplines | Observation that can challenge the link |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Melt extraction → crust composition and density | Pressure, temperature, melt fraction, retained dense residue and porosity | Predicted density or seismic velocity incompatible with observations |
| Crust composition → heat production → later melting | Distribution and depletion of K, Th and U; thermal conductivity; finite inventories | Required heat or melt exceeds the available mass and energy |
| Mantle evolution → core heat loss → dynamo | Core composition, boundary heat flux, time-dependent convection and entropy budget | Field history requires an energy source the evolution model cannot sustain |
| Temperature and reactions → magnetic carriers → remanence | Mineral identity, grain size, redox state, growth time and ambient field | Carrier formed after the proposed acquisition event or cannot survive later heating |
| Water-rock reaction → climate and water inventory | Fluid pathways, water consumption, gas production, degassing and escape | Required fluid throughput or reducing-gas production violates the shared budget |
| Impacts → melt, excavation, shocks and buried sources | Event geometry, depth, cooling and subsequent alteration | Preserved dated material or magnetic sources incompatible with predicted destruction |
| Loading, erosion and deformation → present boundary | Dated deposits, removed volume, fault motion and rotational reference frame | Reconstruction follows different landforms or cannot close its mass balance |

These arrows are proposed interfaces for a common model, not evidence that all branches operated with equal importance on Mars.

## 1. Crust, composition and the heat budget

The density-thickness ambiguity is a gateway to other disciplines. Our existing [physical study](DICHOTOMY_PHYSICS.md) found that the inferred north-south thickness contrast changes substantially across published density assumptions. Surface-wave observations supply additional path-averaged constraints, rather than a direct, uniform seismic survey of both hemispheres. [Kim et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2023GL103482)

Goossens and Sabaka's 2026 producer archive reports different northern and southern densities from a boundary-constrained gravity analysis and supplies alternative constraint strengths and covariance-derived model ensembles. These should be assessed as one inference family. Combining their reported densities with an older thickness map without refitting would not produce a new self-consistent crust. [NASA product and methods](https://pgda.gsfc.nasa.gov/products/102)

Petrology can make that comparison more restrictive. Mackay-Champion et al. interpret the deeper crust beneath InSight as a dense, melt-depleted cumulate layer beneath more mafic material. This is a local interpretation built from seismic velocities and mineral modelling. It does not establish a uniform global layer, nor imply that highlands alone experienced differentiation. [Mackay-Champion et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-026-02907-5)

Bonnet Gibet et al. already couple asymmetric crustal growth and differentiation through thermal feedback. Their parameterized framework allows southern crustal melting under some histories satisfying adopted interior constraints. Its simplified mantle and rheological assumptions must be examined before transferring the result to a particular province. [Bonnet Gibet et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024JE008486)

**Joint consequence proposed here:** use the same retained mineral assemblages and heat-producing-element inventory to predict density, heat production and the supply of magnetic minerals. Independently choosing whichever density, heat budget and carrier abundance fits each dataset would conceal an inconsistent history. The recent post-InSight crust-mantle synthesis is a useful map of these questions, but its integrated estimates remain model-based and its abstract was the material assessed in this pass. [McLennan et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1029/2025RG000882)

## 2. Interior dynamics, the core and the magnetic field

Tidal tomography infers a hotter present southern mantle under a viscoelastic interpretation. Its conversion to temperature depends on composition, rheology and atmospheric/loading corrections. Thermal insulation and interior circulation can both contribute; the observation does not uniquely identify the original trigger. [Berne et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x)

Regional seismic attenuation adds a different observable, although source geometry, scattering and material properties must be disentangled. These analyses partly share InSight's sampling limitations. [Sun and Tkalčić, 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL110921)

The core's boundary conditions also remain active research. A molten silicate layer above the metal core and a reported present solid inner core constrain different aspects of the interior; they should not be treated as interchangeable estimates or presumed mutually exclusive. Neither inference dates the early dynamo. [Samuel et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06601-8); [Bi et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-025-09361-9)

Experiments published in September 2026 investigate the stability of dense basal melts. They supply relevant material physics, not a direct observation of Mars's boundary layer. The publisher's indexed excerpts were consulted; a full methods audit remains outstanding. [Pierru et al., 2026](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-026-02104-z)

Hemispheric ancient fields must remain in the comparison. Yan et al. produce asymmetric fields in full-sphere dynamo simulations with nonuniform core-boundary heat flux. A compatible surface field still needs to be averaged through rock cooling and recording. A current inner-core interpretation does not invalidate an early full-sphere experiment unless the core was already crystallizing at the relevant time. [Yan et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1029/2024GL113926)

**Joint consequence proposed here:** compare shared-dynamo/unequal-recording and unequal-dynamo/explicit-recording branches under common thermal histories. Never substitute a present temperature map directly into a four-billion-year-old acquisition calculation.

## 3. Water, redox chemistry, climate and magnetic memory

A particularly useful connection is that water-rock reactions can affect both atmospheric chemistry and magnetic recording. Adams et al. model transient warming supported by hydrogen associated with crustal hydration and oxidation, with atmospheric composition governing transitions. This is a conditional climate calculation; it does not measure a global past hydrogen flux. [Adams et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-024-01626-8)

Wordsworth et al. separately couple reducing-gas episodes, escape-driven oxidation and surface geochemistry. Their work supplies an alternative formulation against which an integrated scenario should be checked, including its required background pressure. [Wordsworth et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-021-00701-8)

A 4.45-Ga zircon in NWA 7034 contains textural and chemical evidence interpreted as growth in a hydrothermal environment, including magnetite inclusions. The study establishes a mineralogical argument for early fluids in that material; it does not measure a preserved 4.45-Ga paleofield or date a global ocean. [Gillespie et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adq3694)

This makes redox chemistry a bridge worth quantifying. Yet “more iron” is not equivalent to “more stable remanence”: oxidation state, phase, grain structure and reaction history determine recording capacity. Spectral mineral detections map exposed surfaces and require coverage/exposure controls before comparison with deeper magnetic sources. [Magnetic mineralogy textbook chapter](https://pmagpy.github.io/Essentials-JupyterBook/chapter6/); [Carter et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115164)

**Joint consequence proposed here:** follow a finite Fe-water-reaction budget. For each proposed reaction history, ask whether the required minerals, gas fluxes, permeability and heat can coexist. A climate fit cannot independently consume the same reactants already exhausted by the magnetic-mineral scenario. This linked test is a project proposal; its novelty and numerical feasibility have not been established.

## 4. What weak and strong magnetism actually constrain

Orbital fields, surface measurements and laboratory remanence are different measurements. Zhurong's weak field is a valuable local northern constraint, not a census of northern magnetization. [Du et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41550-023-02008-7)

ALH 84001 provides evidence interpreted as an ancient, potentially reversing field, with acquisition tied to impact/thermal history. Simulations show that cooling through reversals can suppress basin-scale fields at spacecraft altitude. This weakens a unique shutdown date inferred from magnetic lows; it does not prove every basin formed during an active dynamo. [Steele et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ade9071); [Steele et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41467-024-51092-4)

The preservation problem also continues on Earth. The nine investigated stones in the NWA 7034 pairing study were magnetically overprinted during handling. Their useful geochemical or chronological evidence must remain separate from compromised natural remanence. [Vervelidou et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1029/2022JE007464)

Our [magnetic evidence dossier](INTERDISCIPLINARY_MAGNETISM.md) turns these distinctions into admission criteria. The immediate scientific target is to identify what each sample or map can constrain, before attempting a global field-strength history.

## 5. Surface evolution, hidden crust and rotational history

Around Mawrth Vallis, remnant mounds and alteration stratigraphy support substantial retreat of the visible highland margin. That regional result distinguishes the present escarpment from a primordial boundary. [McNeil et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41561-024-01634-8)

Nilosyrtis provides a complementary mechanical hypothesis involving gravity-driven extension and shortening. Our existing elastic-profile sensitivity experiment is not a reconstruction of those faults and cannot replace mapped kinematics. [Carboni et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.epsl.2025.119645)

Tharsis growth and true polar wander further affect reconstructed paleogeography. A scenario comparing ancient field geometry, climate latitude and present geological maps must declare its reference frame. The timing and amplitude of reorientation require their own tests. [Bouley et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature17171)

Buried dipping radar reflectors beneath the Zhurong traverse have been interpreted as coastal deposits. This adds a local sedimentary constraint, conditional on the geological interpretation and chronology; it does not establish a synchronous shoreline around the entire planet. [Li et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422213122)

**Joint consequence proposed here:** restore the same dated features, distinguish erosion from deformation and account for burial before asking whether any recovered outline favours an impact geometry.

## 6. Water loss and atmospheric escape

A water history needs separate reservoirs: atmosphere, surface/ice, pore fluids, hydrated minerals, mantle and loss to space. Scheller et al. demonstrate within a model that crustal sequestration can be substantial. Wright et al. infer water-saturated fractured material beneath InSight under a rock-physics model; global extrapolation and alternative pore/composition models remain separate questions. [Scheller et al., 2021](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abc7717); [Wright et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2409983121)

MAVEN observations identify present sputtering signatures. Converting these into an integrated ancient loss requires solar evolution and atmospheric supply, rather than multiplying today's rate by age. An intrinsic magnetic field changes escape pathways, and idealized calculations do not support a universal monotonic shielding law. [Curry et al., 2025](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.adt1538); [Gunell et al., 2018](https://doi.org/10.1051/0004-6361/201832934)

**Joint consequence proposed here:** paleomagnetism constrains one input to an atmospheric history. It is not a direct clock for the disappearance of all liquid water. Lafayette's much later alteration is a useful reminder to keep local fluid events separate from global climate transitions. [Tremblay et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.7185/geochemlet.2443)

## 7. Impact histories and the satellites

A northern excavation origin, a southern crust-production origin and interior amplification must be compared with their actual initial conditions and long-term evolution. The 2008 Borealis reconstruction supplies a geometrical argument; strength-inclusive impact studies and coupled impact-interior calculations investigate different formation paths. Agreement with an outline is only one required outcome. [Andrews-Hanna et al., 2008](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature07011); [Ballantyne et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2022.115395); [Cheng et al., 2024](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.icarus.2024.116137)

If one also assigns Phobos and Deimos to the same impact, the disc mass, angular momentum, composition and satellite evolution become extra constraints. Moon-formation studies allow different impact/disc histories; the moons are not already identified as debris from the dichotomy-forming event. [Rosenblatt et al., 2016](https://doi.org/10.1038/ngeo2742); [Canup and Salmon, 2018](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.aar6887)

## A common comparison, with shared evidence counted once

Use one history to predict the actual observables: gravity coefficients, seismic travel times or dispersion, orbital magnetic vectors at measured altitudes, laboratory components, mineral assemblages and dated surfaces. Some starting models can use published derived products, but their common inputs and assumptions must then be retained.

A future statistical model should separate observation noise, uncertain parameters and model discrepancy. Gravimetric density and thickness from the same inversion are not independent constraints. Magnetic maps from overlapping MGS/MAVEN observations are not independent votes. Paired meteorites and samples from one ejection group are not independent planetary sites. Local chemistry and orbital mineral maps may sample different depths and times.

The next useful calculations are specified in [Scenarios and discriminating tests](INTERDISCIPLINARY_TESTS.md). They connect multiple disciplines from the start, while remaining small enough to validate. Their purpose is to identify contradictions or informative missing observations; no origin probability or best history is assigned by this review.
