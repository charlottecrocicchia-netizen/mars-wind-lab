# One chronology, several kinds of clocks

**Interdisciplinary review · 28 September 2026**

[Synthesis](INTERDISCIPLINARY_SYNTHESIS.md) · [Magnetic evidence](INTERDISCIPLINARY_MAGNETISM.md) · [Tests](INTERDISCIPLINARY_TESTS.md) · [Sources](INTERDISCIPLINARY_SOURCES.md)

Ga and Ma mean billions and millions of years before present. A mineral date, an isotope model age, a crater-count interval and a magnetic acquisition interpretation are different constraints. They must retain their original uncertainties and assumptions. The following is a selected sequence of anchors, **not a continuous global history**.

| Time | Material or event | What constrains it | What it does not date |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| By about 4.547 Ga | An early crustal source inferred from ancient zircons | Hf isotope source modelling in Bouvier et al. | A directly sampled 4.547-Ga rock, the dichotomy or a magnetic field |
| 4.4763–4.4297 Ga | Seven analysed zircons in ancient Martian breccia material | U–Pb mineral dates; the endpoint uncertainties are ±0.9 and ±1.0 Ma as reported in the abstract | Continuous crust production throughout that interval or the age of every breccia component |
| About 4.45 Ga | A hydrothermally interpreted zircon in NWA 7034 | Mineral age plus trace elements and inclusions | A measured paleointensity, a global ocean or all later alteration in the breccia |

| About 4.5 and 3.7 Ga | Magnetic signals associated with selected ancient and volcanic units | Orbital observations combined with geological age assignments | Two direct radiometric dates on the actual orbital magnetic sources |

The zircon range is a range of analysed grains, not a confidence interval on one event. Its endpoint error convention was not independently audited in this pass; it must not silently become 1σ. Lafayette's 741.8 Ma age has ±15.0 Ma analytical precision and ±15.2 Ma external precision, both at 2σ. The accessible paper examines potential thermal resetting using assumed diffusion properties; the date remains linked to that interpretation.

## The missing age is scientifically important

This table intentionally leaves the formation time of the dichotomy and a unique dynamo extinction time unfilled. Assigning either one from an unrelated old mineral or a weak magnetic basin would manufacture a connection. Likewise, a present southern thermal anomaly supplies an endpoint for an evolution model, not a dated initial condition. [Berne et al.](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-026-10893-x)

Isotope reservoirs add another clock: source separation can predate crystallization of a meteorite by billions of years. The divergent histories inferred for different meteorite families constrain mixing and preservation, without identifying an entire hemisphere with any one family. [Borg and Kruijer](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2404257121)

## How to build a history that can be tested

For each proposed crustal parcel, create separate events for formation, cooling, fluid reaction, burial, impact/shock, reheating, exhumation, ejection and terrestrial handling. Only include an event where evidence or the explicit scenario requires it. Attach the clock to its measured material, and attach magnetic acquisition to a mineral generation and a recording mechanism.

The project should then propagate an **ordering graph**: which events must precede others, which may overlap and which remain unconstrained. A phase produced by late alteration cannot carry an earlier remanence without a separately supported inheritance mechanism. A source reservoir's model age is not automatically a grain-growth date. A field recorded after an impact is not automatically the field that existed before the impact.

Test a proposed history against all admissible age ranges; do not force every record into a single timeline before testing whether the samples belong to the same place or reservoir. Geological epoch boundaries and crater chronologies should be chosen explicitly for a future quantitative reconstruction, rather than mixing published scales.
