Keep dense basal material in the mass budget. These 50 km columns are synthetic endmembers; no mineral assemblage or detachment timescale is calculated.

Porosity is uniform in the upper layer, with empty pores. Support is above a shared compensation datum, not an absolute elevation. Mantle density here is fixed at 3,500 kg/m³.

Linear thermal benchmark

Does a small lead become a whole hemisphere?

Degree one is a hemispheric pattern. It grows fastest in this restricted shell model, but nearby spatial scales grow almost as fast. The initial distribution of power matters.

E-folds describe amplification, not elapsed geological time. Power shares depend on the selected initial spectrum and degree cutoff; they are not probabilities for Mars. Large growth extrapolates beyond the linear model.

What is actually solved?

A steady spherical conductive shell, with fixed surface-temperature perturbation and zero basal-flux perturbation. Relative thermal responses represent relative growth only if the other positive linear feedback factors are equal across degrees. There is no evolving mantle convection, melt depletion or saturation.

This tests a restricted part of an existing idea: Bonnet Gibet et al. (2022). We independently implemented the solver; we do not claim a new mechanism.

What would move this forward?

Conserve the finite melt and heat-producing-element inventory, add cooling and saturation, then test whether hemispheric structure survives several initial spectra and convective forcing.

Observed MOLA + assumed loads

Which edge are we trying to restore?

Undo an assumed load and compare the earlier surface it would imply. The picked boundary is only the steepest smoothed descent in 25–45°N. It can switch between different features.

Why this is not yet an ancient boundary map

Every assumed-load case reproduces the present profile exactly by construction. The family has 48 load cases and a no-load control per strip. Neither loading history nor age is measured here. Relief alone cannot select a unique history.

Positive thickness adds a surface load; negative thickness removes it. The model is an elastic plate with a free surface, not a fault-slip or frictional-viscous detachment restoration. The strips are meridional, not necessarily normal to the geological boundary. Numerical resolution is 14.8 km north–south.

Sources: NASA MOLA, Watters (2003), and the regional motivation in Carboni et al. (2025).

Try the ambiguous profile.

Select 56°E, keep the no-load control, and change smoothing from 50 to 100 km. The proxy jumps about 830 km between features. That is a failed feature match, not a tectonic displacement.

What would move this forward?

Track the same dated scarps and deposits across neighboring strips, constrain load history and compare elastic, fault-slip, erosional and viscous explanations.

Learn through a known-input control

A perfect line can give the wrong ancient field.

Natural and laboratory remanence must respond comparably before a slope can become a field estimate. Here, every line is exact. Only the assumed recording efficiency changes.

Exercise: would more points fix this?

More exact points reduce fitting uncertainty but do not fix the unknown efficiency ratio. In this example, apparent field = efficiency ratio × true field. A specific laboratory protocol needs independent controls; a straight line alone cannot validate that conversion.
