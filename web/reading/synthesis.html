What can distinguish the origins of Mars's dichotomy?

For the integrated project argument and its falsifiable first study, start with the working hypothesis and test protocol. This page supplies the broader evidence background.

Research synthesis · 24 September 2026 · provisional source assessment. Read the search method for the distinction between metadata, abstract consultation and selected full-text reading. This synthesis defines tests; it does not report a new inversion or settle the origin debate.

The question is larger than a north–south colour contrast

The target is the relationship between low northern terrain, high southern terrain, crustal structure and geological history. These are related observables, but their boundaries need not coincide everywhere. The southern highlands and northern lowlands are geological provinces, not simply everything south and north of the equator. The visible escarpment has also evolved. Work on boundary stratigraphy and eroded remnants makes the separation between initial formation and later modification essential. Watters et al., 2007; Irwin & Watters, 2010; McNeil et al., 2025.

A useful hypothesis must predict more than the outline on a map: crustal thickness and composition, chronology, thermal state, magnetic recording and subsequent resurfacing. Agreement with one pattern is insufficient if the same model contradicts another independent constraint. Conversely, observations derived from the same gravity field must not be counted as independent votes.

Competing explanations and discriminating observations

Mechanism What the model tries to explain Useful discriminant Important ambiguity
Northern giant impact A large northern cavity and elliptical boundary Reconstructed geometry, surviving crust, impact melt distribution, early age Tharsis removal and initial crust assumptions influence the reconstruction
Southern giant impact and magma pond Excess southern crust created by impact melting Crust production, mantle depletion, long-term volcanic distribution Results change with impact strength, melt extraction and mantle viscosity

Crust–melt thermal feedback Amplification of a small initial thickness difference Growth rate and heat-producing-element partitioning Does not uniquely identify the initial perturbation
Hybrid impact–convection An impact initiates long-lived interior reorganization Joint early impact record and later mantle/crust evolution Flexible models can fit many observations without a decisive prediction
Early crustal recycling / plate tectonics Removal and renewal of northern crust Coherent structural, age, geochemical and magnetic predictions Magnetic stripes alone are not plate-boundary evidence
Erosion, deposition and volcanism Modification or concealment of an older boundary Buried surfaces, stratigraphy and erosional volume Later resurfacing is not automatically the cause of the original crustal contrast

The northern-impact family is quantified by the 2008 gravity/topography reconstruction and impact calculations. Their compatibility with an impact is strong motivation to test the scenario, not a direct observation of an impactor. Andrews-Hanna et al.; Marinova et al.; Nimmo et al..

Strength-inclusive simulations and coupled long-term models instead find conditions under which an impact builds the southern highlands through crust production. The location of the impacted hemisphere is therefore itself a model question. Compare assumptions and predictions rather than selecting a model by its title. Ballantyne et al., 2023; Cheng et al., 2024.

Viscosity layering can favour degree-one convection, while thermal insulation and melt extraction can amplify crustal asymmetry. These models supply measurable requirements on timescales, rheology and heat production. They must survive comparison with early crustal ages. Roberts & Zhong, 2006; Bonnet Gibet et al., 2022. The historical plate-tectonic proposal remains in the comparison as a testable alternative, not an established reconstruction. Sleep, 1994.

Interior structure: a real contrast, a nonunique history

Gravity/topography models anchored by InSight constrain global crustal thickness, but the answer depends on density and the local seismic layering. Wieczorek et al.'s model family permits mean thicknesses of roughly 30–72 km. Surface-wave analysis subsequently favours a narrower 42–56 km mean in its tested model space. These intervals describe different analyses and assumptions, not repeated direct measurements of one scalar. Wieczorek et al., 2022; Kim et al., 2023.

Attenuation differences along northern and southern source paths suggest thermal structure, but path geometry, source effects and scattering complicate a direct temperature interpretation. A single lander still provides a restricted sampling geometry. Sun & Tkalčić; Drilleau et al., 2024.

A 2026 tidal-gravity inversion supports a hotter southern mantle and tests atmospheric-loading corrections. It explicitly leaves room for interior circulation or insulation by thicker crust. This is evidence about the present interior, not a unique record of the original formation event. Berne et al., 2026. Thermal evolution models already show that unequal crust and radiogenic heating can create unequal mantle temperatures. Thiriet et al., 2018.

Recent lower-crustal petrological modelling beneath InSight illustrates another issue: a seismic discontinuity can reflect differentiation within the crust, rather than a simple universal crust–mantle boundary. Local lithological interpretations must be propagated through global models with uncertainty. Mackay-Champion et al., 2026.

Core models also evolve. The early large-core interpretation, a later molten silicate layer above a smaller core, and the 2025 interpretation of an approximately 600-km solid inner core change permissible thermal histories. Do not average these model-dependent estimates or confuse today's structure with the core four billion years ago. Stähler et al., 2021; Samuel et al., 2023; Bi et al., 2025, with its Methods-reference correction.

Magnetism: the signal has several causes

MGS and MAVEN reveal spatially heterogeneous remanent crustal fields. Orbital measurements integrate sources over depth and suppress small-scale structure. A map evaluated closer to the ground is still a model with finite bandwidth, not a new surface measurement. Acuña et al., 1999; Langlais et al., 2019. Zhurong's weak local field along a Utopia traverse adds a valuable northern surface constraint but cannot represent all northern crust. Du et al., 2023.

A weak observed anomaly can result from a weak acquisition field, few carriers, excavation, thermal or shock demagnetization, deep burial, or cancellation among sources. Hemispheric dynamo models add another possibility: the original field itself was asymmetric. Stanley et al., 2008; Lillis et al., 2013; AlHantoobi et al., 2021.

Polarity reversals during cooling can yield weak basin-scale orbital signals even while a dynamo operates. The published experiments cover basins far smaller than Borealis, so their outcome cannot simply be assigned to the entire northern lowlands. Steele et al., 2024. Geological interpretation of magnetic signatures also motivates dynamo activity beyond a single traditional shutdown date. Mittelholz et al., 2020.

Project inference: testing the dichotomy requires a forward model of recording and preservation, not only a comparison between the amplitudes of two hemispheres. This follows from the competing mechanisms above; it is a proposed analysis, not a result already obtained here.

Meteorites: essential evidence with a biased sampling geometry

ALH 84001, nakhlites, shergottites and regolith breccias illuminate different times, lithologies and recording processes. Paired stones and common ejection groups are not independent geographical samples. Candidate source craters can improve geological context, but their inferred provenance should remain probabilistic. Udry et al., 2020; Lagain et al., 2022; Herd et al., 2024.

Ancient zircon U–Pb ages and Hf reservoir modelling constrain early crust extraction and reworking. They do not directly date the visible hemispheric boundary. A shock feature in an old zircon likewise dates an event only when its resetting history is established; the grain's formation age is not automatically its shock age. Bouvier et al., 2018; Cox et al., 2022.

The most urgent magnetic quality issue is collection history. Hand magnets have remagnetized the nine studied stones of the NWA 7034 pairing group. Those natural-remanence measurements cannot be treated as intact Martian paleofields. Carrier mineralogy can still inform crustal analogues; it answers a different question. Vervelidou et al., 2023; Gattacceca et al., 2014. See the dedicated meteorite dossier for preservation criteria and sample-level distinctions.

Water, surface modification and atmosphere

Clay-bearing exposures and geological mapping along the boundary constrain alteration, substrate and later resurfacing. These are useful tests of an evolutionary scenario but may substantially postdate the initial dichotomy. Spectral non-detection can reflect dust, exposure or resolution. Boundary clay study, 2026; Southern Utopia mapping, 2025.

Proposed northern oceans require careful elevation, deformation and age tests. Regional delta elevations can instead support separate lakes; that result does not eliminate every ocean hypothesis. Rivera-Hernández & Palucis, 2019. Meteorite hydrogen isotopes constrain water-reservoir models but also depend on exchange and fractionation assumptions. Kurokawa et al., 2014.

The causal story “dynamo stopped, atmosphere disappeared, dichotomy followed” is too compressed. Magnetic topology, solar forcing, volatile supply and loss pathways interact. A weak dipole can increase ion escape in particular simulations; neither universal protection nor universal enhanced escape follows. Sakai et al., 2018. Present atmospheric circulation is primarily a constraint on present conditions and observation corrections. It cannot alone identify a primordial crust-forming event.

A productive way to combine the evidence

Use at least three independent families: crust/geometry, dated rock histories, and magnetic/thermal evolution. Within each, track shared data, spatial resolution and model assumptions. A magnetic map and a paper interpreting that same map are one observational lineage. Several stones from one meteorite pairing group are one sampling lineage. Gravity inversion, seismic interpretation and an inferred thermal history are linked layers of inference, not three direct temperature measurements.

The most defensible near-term outcome is a set of excluded parameter combinations and robust predictions. For example: can an impact scenario generate the required crustal contrast while preserving old crustal reservoirs and producing the observed distribution of magnetic anomalies after realistic recording and resurfacing? Can an internal-growth scenario do the same within the allowed early timescale? No numerical odds are assigned here because the necessary common likelihoods, uncertainty model and comparable simulation ensemble have not been built.

The research programme specifies executable next analyses and their failure tests. The mission inventory identifies public archive entry points; it is an acquisition map, not a claim that every mission product has already been processed.
