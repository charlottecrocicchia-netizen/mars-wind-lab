Competing histories and tests that connect disciplines

Interdisciplinary review · 28 September 2026

Synthesis · Chronology · Magnetic evidence · Sources

These are proposed tests, not executed results. The existing physical experiments, regional study and magnetic controls remain separately labelled. This review assigns no probability to a formation mechanism.

Keep formation and recording as separate model choices

Formation family Published starting point Main joint burden of explanation Observation that could challenge a specified realization
Northern impact and northern crustal removal/reworking Andrews-Hanna et al. 2008 Recovered geometry, excavation/melting, later loading and surviving old material Reconstructed structural boundary or preserved material incompatible with that event's predicted geometry and heating

Impact seed plus internal amplification A project comparison branch using the above model families A dated trigger and a finite, explicitly parameterized response Extra adjustable stages improve individual fits but make incompatible predictions for shared material and energy budgets

A hybrid is not automatically the most plausible explanation. It needs defined parameters, a comparison with simpler alternatives and predictions that can fail. “Northern impact” and “southern impact” also summarize families; differences between simulations must not be reduced to a single preferred impact point.

For each formation family, compare at least two recording branches: a broadly shared dynamo with unequal rock recording/preservation, and an asymmetric dynamo with explicit rock recording. Variable or reversing fields can be tested within these branches. Surface erosion, burial and alteration are later events constrained separately, not free adjustments after seeing every residual.

Test 1 — One composition must satisfy gravity, seismic structure and heat

Question: can the same admissible mineral assemblages, porosity and heat-producing-element distributions explain the crustal contrast and its thermal evolution?

Predict density and elastic properties at the relevant pressure and temperature; enforce a common crust/mantle elemental inventory. Compare with local and path-averaged seismic constraints and alternative gravity-derived crustal models. Keep density–thickness covariance, porosity and vertical layering explicit. Wieczorek et al.; Kim et al.; Mackay-Champion et al.

Discriminating outcome: a proposed crustal history fails if no parameter set within its declared bounds can reproduce these observables with one mass and heat budget. A fit is a consistency result, not proof of the origin. The existing layered-density calculation is a starting sensitivity experiment; it does not already perform this joint test.

Needed before execution: approved petrological inputs, covariance information and a documented mapping from local seismology to the global model. Do not simply insert a new density into an old thickness map and call it a self-consistent solution.

Test 2 — One reaction history must satisfy water, hydrogen and magnetic minerals

Question: can candidate water–rock reactions supply the mineral changes and reducing-gas flux needed by a chosen magnetic/climate history, within the available fluid, reactant and energy budgets?

Choose explicit reactions and carrier generations; track stoichiometric consumption, iron oxidation state, permeability and thermal conditions. Couple the resulting gas supply to a specified atmosphere model and distinguish recirculated fluid throughput from net water consumption. Compare with mineral detections, dated alteration and source-depth constraints. Adams et al.; Wordsworth et al.; Scheller et al.

Discriminating outcome: reject a specified history if it requires unavailable reactants, contradictory mineral generations, incompatible temperatures or an impossible net water inventory. Success would demonstrate feasibility, not establish that those reactions occurred at the required global rate. No reaction network or climate simulation has been run in this review.

Needed before execution: independently supported reaction parameters, source volumes, fluid pathways and chronological overlap. This bridge is already represented in the literature; the project must establish what additional constraint its proposed calculation contributes.

Test 3 — Compare magnetic predictions in matched geological regions

Question: after controlling for altitude, unit, age, exposure, burial and source depth, which proposed recording history predicts held-out observations?

Use spatial blocks and compare regions with comparable observation coverage. Retain multiple admissible field/source models, but track their shared MGS/MAVEN input rather than treating them as independent replications. An absent spectral detection is not an unaltered-rock label. Run uncoupled magnetic/gravity interpretations before testing an explicit coupling prior. Langlais et al.; Carter et al.; Mittelholz et al.

Discriminating outcome: a proposed transferable recording relation fails if it reverses under defensible matching choices or consistently predicts held-out regions worse than the declared baseline. The existing Newton sensitivity result motivates this control; it does not determine the physical cause of that region's contrast.

Needed before execution: coverage masks, documented regional selection, admissible geological ages and reuse rights for any new inputs. Current project data can support limited sensitivity work; a new global mineral product is not assumed available for unrestricted reuse.

Test 4 — Make every scenario preserve the dated material it requires

Question: does the complete thermal, impact and fluid history preserve the mineral and isotope records used to support it?

Build event graphs by material and region. Predict cooling and reheating, then evaluate mechanism-specific survival rather than using one universal blocking temperature. Keep uncertainty in acquisition ages and alternative local-field explanations. Bouvier et al.; Borg and Kruijer; Steele et al. 2023

Discriminating outcome: a realization fails if its required events cannot satisfy the admitted age ordering or it predicts destruction of a record whose preservation is independently supported. The project's conductive column can exclude some thermal histories, but it does not calculate chemical alteration, shock physics or all magnetic relaxation processes.

Needed before execution: source-specific kinetic parameters, mineral generations and supported event associations. Uncertain pairing or source-crater assignments remain branches, not fixed geographic anchors.

Test 5 — Reconstruct the boundary before fitting its origin

Question: can one history explain present relief, deeper structure and the independently mapped evidence of retreat and deformation?

Separate escarpments, buried structural boundaries and magnetic transitions. Restore loading, regional erosion/deposition, mapped faults and any declared rotational history. Track removed and deposited volumes. Compare predicted geometries without choosing a different feature after each failed fit. McNeil et al.; Carboni et al.; Bouley et al.

Discriminating outcome: a reconstruction fails if it cannot close the material balance or requires deformation inconsistent with mapped kinematics. A visually elliptical present outline is insufficient. The existing four MOLA strips only illustrate sensitivity to feature selection.

Needed before execution: reproducible feature definitions, uncertainty on buried structure, dated regional geology and realistic flexural/deformational physics.

Which evidence is genuinely additional?

Shared evidence family Records that can overlap Consequence for comparison
Gravity/topography Density, crustal thickness, some structural reconstructions Preserve common input and covariance; do not multiply nominally separate likelihoods
InSight Crust, mantle, core and some water interpretations Separate observables and modelling assumptions; retain shared station and event limitations
MGS/MAVEN magnetics Global maps, localized spectra and regional anomalies Model variation measures robustness, not independent sampling of Mars
Meteorite suites Paired stones, repeated analyses, common source/ejection groups Distinguish specimen, rock, event and planetary-region replication
Orbital imaging/spectroscopy Surface age, alteration, boundary mapping Track coverage, exposure, resolution and common geological assignments
Integrated reviews Estimates assembled from the preceding families Use to organize evidence; do not add as a new measurement

A future joint model should compare predicted measurements to observations using a covariance/discrepancy model, then test sensitivity to reasonable priors and held-out evidence. This is a proposed analysis architecture. The present review contains no fabricated numerical confidence score and no claim to have evaluated its computational feasibility in full.

The most useful first deliverable is a shared material-and-event record feeding Tests 1, 2 and 4, alongside a matched-region evaluation for Test 3. That keeps the research interdisciplinary from the beginning while making failures traceable. The origin comparison should be updated only after these tests yield actual results.
