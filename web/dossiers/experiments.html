READING DOSSIER · 28 September 2026

Earlier experiments

These studies explain why the current six tests need controls. Their saved results and interactive settings remain available, but the current report is the entry point for conclusions.

Spatial association is not regional prediction

The earlier map workshop compares dispersed held-out cells with omitted longitude wedges. Performance changes when nearby training information disappears. Test 1 extends that question to geological epochs and hemisphere indicators. Shift controls diagnose spatial alignment; they are not automatically valid permutation p-values.

A rock records a time window

The recording benchmark and thermal workshop show how cooling and reheating select parts of a field history. Tests 2 and 4 apply this idea to accepted conductive histories. The detection workshop separates seeing a signal from identifying its history; this helps interpret the amplitude and surface comparisons in Tests 3 and 5.

A good fit is not an ancient age

The laboratory control includes known contaminated material. The paleointensity exercise varies recording efficiency. Both constrain how strongly source requirements in Test 3 can be interpreted as rock properties. Neither adds a new planetary verdict.

Geometry and reference terrain matter

The regional pilot explores crater interiors and reference rings. Its contrasts depend on sampling choices. Test 5 adds a different limitation: an orbital model does not resolve all local ground fields. The crustal support workshop prepares the density–thickness question now treated by the full inversion in Test 6.

Amplification and boundary-restoration workshops remain exploratory controls, not independent confirmation of a six-test verdict. The archived methods below keep their own reproduction commands, input records and earlier conclusions.
