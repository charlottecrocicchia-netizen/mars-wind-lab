READING DOSSIER · 28 September 2026

Interdisciplinary review

The goal is to make the disciplines constrain one another. A plausible magnetic history should also fit crustal structure, geological timing, mineral properties and thermal evolution. Agreement requires comparing the same places, depths and times.

Keep the clocks separate

Crystallization, impact heating, alteration, surface emplacement and magnetic acquisition are different events. An isotopic age can constrain a magnetic component only through a supported event history. The chronology review documents these distinctions; the ALH 84001 workshop retains unknown acquisition ages for its own candidate fits.

Match the spatial scale

An orbital field, a geological unit, a deep source model and a meteorite sample do not observe the same volume. Test 5 makes the local-versus-orbital discrepancy concrete. Matching a map grid to another grid does not create independent observations or recover unresolved structure.

Ask for a joint prediction

An archive scenario must explain both where a record survives and how much magnetization remains. Cooling that permits acquisition may also average over reversals. A material that carries enough remanence must fit a plausible composition and bulk density. The follow-up protocols now distinguish the executed material, cooling and identifiability calculations from unexecuted stages.

Read the evidence trail

The preserved synthesis links the core, mantle, crust, magnetic carriers, water and atmosphere. Its annotated ledger states the reading depth for articles and books. Its earlier five joint-test proposals are a separate planning layer from the six executed tests and six current follow-ups.

A cross-disciplinary narrative is useful when it exposes incompatible predictions or missing inputs. The number of disciplines mentioned does not itself strengthen an inference.
