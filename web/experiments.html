The rock records a time window, not the whole history.

Each illustrative blocking temperature records the field at its last cooling crossing. Later heating can reset it. The bands below are assumptions, not measured mineral spectra.

Known newly acquired contribution only. Bars show ensemble medians; whiskers are 5–95% phase/history quantiles, not confidence intervals. A single deterministic case has no ensemble spread. Reversing labels give the chron duration (the mean for Poisson histories). Absolute value is taken after signed contributions are summed.

Borrow a signal-processing view: the acquisition filter

A sinusoidal input reveals which temporal variations this recording window transmits. Unity preserves amplitude; zero means complete cancellation of that component. The gain is normalized over the known acquisition kernel and excludes unknown prehistory.

Computed with exact interval integration on the piecewise-linear thermal history. This is a linear relay benchmark; grain relaxation, mineral reactions and multidirectional fields are still missing.

Inspect geometry, units and numerical checks

Ten 1 km thick layers from 20 to 30 km depth form a 150 km radius cylinder. Reference magnetization is 10 A/m. Fields are exact on-axis values for this flat, uniformly magnetized geometry; they are not predictions for a mapped Martian basin. Field times are elapsed Myr, not ages before present.

SYNTHETIC BENCHMARK / DETECTION AND IDENTIFICATION

Detecting a field does not identify its history.

First verify a known-template detector with injected signals. Then ask whether the four-height field pattern distinguishes a history from a scaled steady-field model.

8,192 independent Gaussian trials per signal-to-noise level; fixed known template and a 1% two-sided false-alarm threshold. This is an ideal detector benchmark, not an estimate of mission sensitivity.

A nuisance parameter changes the question.

Mineral abundance and recording efficiency can change amplitude. We therefore fit a free signed scale to the steady-field prediction before comparing patterns. A small residual means that this particular geometry and noise level cannot separate those cases by shape.

Reference comparison: reheating, 250–550 °C blocking band, four heights (10, 50, 150, 400 km), independent noise σ = 5 nT per height. Unknown prehistory is excluded from these conditional values. For zero-signal cases, 1% is the fixed detector’s false-alarm rate.

How altitude filters spatial structure

An independent single-degree spherical-harmonic check uses the exact amplitude factor (R / (R + h))^(l + 2). Higher degree means finer spatial structure. This spherical check and the flat cylinder are separate models; their predictions are not combined.

EXISTING DATA / PREDICTING OMITTED REGIONS

Does a relationship transfer across Mars?

Predict the saved magnetic-field model from location, crustal structure and surface geology. Compare dispersed test points with entirely omitted longitude wedges. The second task tests regional transfer and may require extrapolation.

Area-weighted skill on log(1 + |B| in nT), relative to each fold’s training-only mean. Zero equals that baseline; negative is worse. Dependent altitude/density variants are sensitivity checks, not additional independent observations.

What is held out, fitted and controlled?

Six 60° longitude wedges, a 10° training buffer on each side, and |latitude| ≤ 75°. The buffer is angular, not a constant physical distance. All scaling uses training data only. Ridge strength is fixed at 0.01; no search selected the best split or penalty. The model uses 13,680 retained grid centers; they are not independent samples.

Location uses smooth degree-one/two Cartesian functions on the sphere. Structure uses elevation, crust thickness, their squares and interaction, and mapped geological units. Combined uses both. The target is a derived field model, not independent spacecraft tracks.

Longitude-shift controls preserve latitudinal structure but are not exchangeable null hypotheses. They expose alternative spatial alignments; they do not produce causal significance or p-values.

EXISTING DATA / CANDIDATE COMPONENTS AND CONTROLS

A straight demagnetization path is not a date.

Reanalyze fixed treatment windows using principal-component fits, a fit forced through the origin, and removal of one treatment at a time. Known-contaminated paired stones provide a useful control.

Cartesian moments normalized to the largest retained mean vector in this specimen. Repeated treatment levels are averaged as vectors. These are archived specimen coordinates, not Martian geographic directions. Lines connect treatment steps; they are not component fits.

MAD measures line scatter. The other angles measure sensitivity to forcing the fit through zero, leaving out one treatment, or retaining the last repeat. None is an age, a paleointensity, or a probability that the component is ancient.
