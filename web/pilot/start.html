What happened to the magnetic record?

A first study connecting orbital observations, geological context and the histories recorded by Martian rocks.

OUR WORKING QUESTION

Different ancient fields,
or different ways to preserve them?

A magnetic map records a long history: rock formation, cooling, impacts, mineral growth and later alteration. Before interpreting the north–south contrast, we test how reliably a regional magnetic contrast can describe that history.

This first study compares four craters in three regions, plus a flagged window near Ladon. It tests the measurements and their sensitivity before selecting a physical explanation.

THE FIRST RESULT

The reference terrain can change the answer.

Newton looks weaker than its surroundings. Compare the same mapped surface units, and that contrast reverses. Schiaparelli changes sign with observation altitude.

These are useful constraints on how to construct a physical test. They do not identify the cause of the crustal dichotomy.
