REGIONAL PILOT · 03 / 05

Three histories. Different predictions.

Separate what could have happened from what would be needed to demonstrate it.

This pilot does not distinguish the three histories.

The regional calculation measures an orbital contrast. It cannot date a magnetic component or identify the mineral generation carrying it.
H1 / PRESERVATION

An early record survives

Ancient carriers retain a remanence acquired before later geological events.

Inspect ALH 84001 →
What this history must explain
The age and stability of an old magnetic component, the surviving carrier population and later heating or alteration.
What could contradict it
Evidence that the carrier formed after the proposed recording event, or that subsequent conditions destroyed that component.
What we find here
Lower orbital amplitudes do not establish loss of remanence. Source geometry, thickness and vector cancellation remain alternatives.

Status: unresolved. No regional survival history has been fitted.

H2 / THERMAL RESETTING

A later event rewrites part of the record

Heating or shock changes which grains retain an older component; cooling can record another field.

Inspect Tissint →
What this history must explain
A measured blocking spectrum and time–temperature path compatible with both reset and preserved components.
What could contradict it
A thermal history unable to reset the component, or one that would erase independent mineral or isotopic evidence that survives.
What we find here
Copernicus and Huygens remain weaker than their comparison rings across the tested choices, but no temperatures or reset ages follow from those ratios.

Status: unresolved. Present-day thermal endpoints are not a history of heating.

H3 / CHEMICAL REBUILDING

Water–rock reactions change the recorder

Alteration can create, transform or destroy magnetic carriers. The outcome also depends on the field present during the reaction.

Inspect Lafayette's separate clocks →
What this history must explain
A dated mineral generation, reaction conditions and a magnetic component linked to that generation.
What could contradict it
A remanence older than the mineral carrying it, or a reaction path that cannot produce the required carrier under independently constrained conditions.
What we find here
Surface mineral detections alone cannot establish alteration of deep magnetic sources. The missing survey/exposure mask prevents a controlled mineral–field association test.

Status: unresolved. No chemical acquisition efficiency or alteration age is inferred.

Where does the dichotomy fit?

These histories can occur within impact-driven or internally generated crustal evolution. The pilot tests the reliability of one part of that larger argument: interpreting the magnetic record. It does not rank those global origin scenarios.
